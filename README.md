<h1 align="center">Varv one</h1>

**Varv one** is a loop station that runs in the browser, built for the phone. You listen to the click and your loops in Bluetooth headphones and record with the phone's own microphone. Bluetooth delays everything you hear by a couple of hundred milliseconds, so anything you play along is recorded late. Varv corrects that: every recording is shifted back by a latency you set, or have the app measure for you, so it lands on the beat.

*Varv* is Swedish for lap, or one turn round the loop.

**Open it:** https://olleviotti.github.io/varv/. On an iPhone, open it in Safari, allow the microphone, and choose Add to Home Screen from the share menu. It then runs full screen, works offline, and keeps your saved projects.

## Guide

Short guide lines on the play screen, in edit mode and in settings explain each part when it first becomes relevant, one short line at a time on the play screen, and each one disappears for good once you have used what it describes, or when you tap it. Settings has a button to show them again.

## The play view, and what slides over it

The top bar is a single row: the bar and beat counter on the left, the Varv logo in the middle and the in and out meters on the right. Its bottom edge is amber while playing and the whole bar turns red while recording. Everything else slides over the play view and leaves a strip of it showing; tapping the strip, swiping back or tapping the logo returns to play.

- **Play** is the live view, with a ring next to the lap counter that fills as the lap goes round. Each track has its own colour: on the left a tile with **M** above **S**, its coloured edge pulsing with the track's level; then a dark panel with the track's number and its waveform in its colour and the playhead (an empty track shows an empty panel, and a track you can't hear fades out); and on the right the sample button and the record button. Tracks are numbered by their place in the list, not named. A new project starts with four empty tracks; the tracks share the screen height and shrink as more are added, down to a minimum, after which the list scrolls.
  - **Microphone**: records a sample (tap again, or press Record, to stop), which makes the track a sampler. On a sampler the button shows a keyboard and opens its pads. It is hidden on a track that holds microphone recordings.
  - **M** (top of the tile) mutes the track and **S** (bottom) solos it, following the mute timing.
  - **●** records one lap into the track, starting at its next lap: the button blinks while it waits and turns red while it records, and the track says "next lap", then "recording". When stopped, playback starts with the count-in first. Tap it again to cancel.
  - **Swipe right on a track** (or touch it and slide right) to edit that track.
- **Swipe right for Edit.** It slides in from the left, with Master and then the track names as tabs along its top; it opens on the track you last touched. Each track shows, in the order you need them live: **Delete**, which removes the track, **Empty**, which removes the recordings or laps and keeps every setting (a sampler keeps its sample), **Reset**, which also sets everything back as on a new track, the sample included (all three ask Sure? while counting down 3, 2, 1, and the bar at the bottom can bring back what they removed), Mute and Solo, on a row in the order Delete, Reset, Empty, Mute, Solo that stays pinned under the tabs while you scroll, with a Play button below it on a sampler to hear the sample with its current edits; **Live** (volume, filter, reverb and delay); the **Takes**, or for a sampler the **Sample** and its **Laps**, newest first, each with its own waveform, a switch to silence it without removing it, its latency, Reverse and Remove; then sections that fold open and shut with an arrow: on a sampler **Sample and envelope**, **Compressor**, **Pitch** and **Autotune**; the **Takes** or **Laps**; **EQ and pan** (with the filter slider again, the same one as under Live), **Distortion**, on a microphone track **Pitch** and **Autotune**, and **Bars**. Live is always shown, without an arrow. The others are folded, except that a section opens by itself when something in it differs from the default for the track being edited: change track 1's EQ and EQ and pan is open whenever you edit track 1, but not track 2. Opening or closing a section by hand lasts only while you stay on that track; switch track or leave Edit and the sections follow the rule again. Master works the same way. A track becomes a sampler when a sample is recorded on it, and a microphone track when audio is recorded into it. Master has Live (volume, filter, reverb and delay sends) and folded EQ, Delay, Reverb and Compressor. Double-tap any setting to return it to its default: sliders, EQ handles and switches anywhere, and the number on a stepper (tempo, beats, bars, latency). Settings locked by recordings stay as they are.
- **Swipe left for Settings,** which slide in from the right, with **Audio settings** at the top (it opens the audio page), then **Loop and click**, always shown, and sections that fold open and shut with an arrow, folded until you open them, and staying as you leave them: **Projects and files** (the current project, the saved projects in a fold of their own with how many there are, and the performance recordings); **Recording, mute and scenes** (overdub, and when mute, solo and scenes take effect); and **Help** (the guide and the keys).

## Latency correction

- **Per recording.** Every recording is shifted back by the latency in force when it was made. Each one keeps its own value, which you can nudge afterwards in steps of 1 or 10 ms while the loop plays, watching the hits line up with the beat lines.
- **Automatic measurement, exact.** The app plays ten clicks and listens for them through the microphone, using a matched filter tuned to the click, so no human timing is involved. Hold the phone's microphone against an earbud and tap Measure exactly. It finds a faint click buried in noise and reports nothing rather than a wrong value when it cannot hear the clicks.
- **Screen latency, for the sampler.** A note played on the screen is late by the headphone delay plus the time the touch takes to reach the app. Measure it once with **Measure by tapping**: after a count-in of four higher clicks the app plays eight clicks and you tap along on the beat as you hear it. Nothing on the screen moves in time with the clicks, since the screen has a delay of its own, so you tap only to what you hear. If the audio or the microphone has only just started, it first waits until they have settled (an iPhone can still be switching its audio route), so the first clicks do not glitch. Taps far from the rest are dropped and the others averaged; the result says how much your taps scattered and how precise the value is. Touches are placed on the audio clock through the offset between the page's clock and the audio clock, measured by the app itself: the freshest of the last second of readings of the audio clock. The browser's own estimate of its output latency is not used, since on a phone with Bluetooth it is not known to stay put. It sits under the headphone latency in Settings and can be nudged in 1 ms steps; until it is measured it is guessed as the headphone latency plus 60 ms. The headphone latency starts at 185 ms; double-tap either value to return it to its default.
- **Restart audio**, on the audio page under Restart and more, closes the sound and the microphone completely and starts them again, as when the app opens; recordings, samples and settings stay. Try it if the delay grows, the latency measurements start to vary, or the sound goes to the wrong place; then measure the latency again.
- **Audio setup.** Every audio setting lives on one page: tap the in and out meters at the top (or the A key, or Audio settings at the top of Settings). It has the microphone, its gain and low cut, and the output, each with a large meter, and a test sound; **Latency** (both measurements); and **Restart and more** (restart audio, auto level recordings and what is in use). The output can be chosen where the browser allows it: directly in Chrome and Edge; on iPhone with iOS 26 the whole mix is then played through one media element whose output can be chosen, since Safari cannot choose the output of Web Audio itself, which adds a little delay; System default always plays directly. Elsewhere the system chooses, and on iPhone the output is changed from Control Center. Without headphones an iPhone plays through the earpiece while the microphone is on, because Safari records in the phone's call mode; choose the speaker on the audio page to hear it out loud. The microphones and the outputs are tappable rows with a tick on the one in use, in Settings too; iOS only allows the output to be switched inside a tap. The latency depends on the output, so measure it again after changing it.
- **Microphone choice.** The iPhone microphone is the default, even when headphones with a microphone are connected, and the app switches back to it if the system changes the input. On some iPhones and iPads, Safari moves the sound to the speaker when the phone's microphone starts; Varv then sets the audio session again, which brings the sound back to the headphones. `routetest.html` is a small page for checking how a device routes the sound. Another microphone, such as a USB interface, can be chosen in Settings.

## Loop length

- **Bars.** Tempo 40 to 240 bpm with tap tempo, 1 to 16 beats per bar and 1 to 32 bars per lap, with a click and a one-bar count-in.
- **Bars per track.** In Edit each track can have its own number of bars, for example a 4-bar melody over a 1-bar beat. It records on its own boundaries and its strip shows its own bars and playhead. The setting is fixed once the track holds a recording.
- **Free.** No tempo and no click. The first Record starts at once; play, then tap **End loop** to set the length (up to 30 seconds). Every track then has that length. Since nothing is playing during that first recording, it is not shifted for latency; later recordings are, so they land on the first one.

## Recording

- **One button.** Tap Record and it records lap after lap, each lap into the next empty track (or a new one), until you tap Record again; the button then reads **Finish**, which makes the lap in progress the last (pressed just as a lap ends, the lap just taken is the last); after that it reads **Keep going**, which takes it back. The track waiting for the next lap is marked **auto**. A lap with nothing played in it is thrown away and the same track waits for the next lap, so the tracks fill in order, top down. Hold Record to start or stop playback. This all works from a single pedal button.

- **A track's ● button** instead records exactly one lap into that track.
- **Capture last** keeps the lap you just played even if you never pressed Record.
- **Undo** removes the latest recording. Removed recordings and deleted tracks stay in a bar at the bottom with a Restore button until you dismiss it.
- **Levels.** Input gain (−12 to +30 dB) and an optional 80 Hz low cut sit before the recording. Recordings are kept at the level they were played; automatic levelling is an option. The in and out meters in the top bar turn amber near full scale, red on clipping or when the output limiter has to work hard. Each track shows its own level under its name.
- A live strip shows what the microphone hears, already shifted by the current latency.

## Tracks

- Add tracks before you start, name them and give them their own effects.
- **A track's ● button** records into it from its next lap. The Record button and the pedal fill the empty tracks from the top, then create new ones.
- Recording again into a track that already holds audio replaces what was there, from the moment the new take starts playing; the earlier takes stay on the track, silent (dimmed in Edit), and Undo brings the previous one back. With **Overdub** on in Settings, the new take is added on top instead. Each take has its own latency, reverse and remove.
- **Mute timing:** mute and solo act immediately by default. In Settings they can instead land on the next beat or the next bar (in free mode: the next pass of the loop), so you can tap a little ahead.
- Per track, in Edit: name, bars, volume, pan, graphical EQ, filter (darker or thinner), delay send and reverb send, plus the waveform and each take's latency, reverse and remove.
- **Distortion** per track, in Edit (folded, off until switched on): Soft rounds the peaks like an overdriven tube, Hard clips them, Fuzz bends the two halves of the wave differently for a rougher sound. Drive goes up to +40 dB, and the output level is evened out so more drive gives more grit rather than more volume. Tone is a lowpass after it (800 Hz to 12 kHz), Mix blends in the clean sound and Level sets the output. It sits before the track's filter and EQ, is oversampled 4x so the new harmonics do not fold back, and works on microphone and sampler tracks alike.
- **Transpose** a track in Edit, under Pitch: octaves (±2), semitones (±11) and a fine tuner in cents (±50). The tempo and the timing stay the same. Every recording on the track is shifted, and the play screen shows the setting under the track name. Double-tap a value to reset it. The shift is computed in the background from the stored recording, so changing it loses nothing; the old version keeps playing until the new one is ready.
- **Autotune** a track in Edit, under Autotune: it pulls a single voice or instrument to the nearest note of a key and scale (all 12 notes by default, or major, minor or major pentatonic). **Speed** is how fast a note is pulled in (hard gives the robotic effect, slower keeps vibrato and slides) and **Amount** how far. The key is what you hear, after any transposing. A note is held until another is clearly closer, so a voice between two notes does not flicker. Chords confuse it.

## Sampler

A track becomes a sampler when a sample is recorded on it: tap the microphone button on an empty track's row (or **+ Sampler** on the pads), play or sing one sound, and tap again (or press Record) to stop; it stops by itself after 8 seconds. Reset in Edit makes it an empty track again. On a sampler, **New** in Edit records a new sample in its place.

- **Auto trim** cuts the sample as soon as it is recorded. The start goes back from the loudest point only to where the sound falls below a tenth of it, then 2 ms before the attack, so breaths and noise before the sound are cut; the end is where the level, averaged over 30 ms, is back down at the noise heard before the sound, plus 10 ms. The trim view shows the whole recording with the kept part lit; drag either edge to trim by hand, double-tap it or tap Auto trim to trim automatically again.
- **Pitch.** The pitch of the sample is detected when it is recorded, as a note with cents (YIN, as for autotune), so the keys play in tune from it. Sounds without a clear pitch, like most drums, count as C4. The root note can be moved by hand in semitones; double-tap it to go back to the detected one.
- **Edit options act on the sample.** On a sampler, the pitch (octaves, semitones and fine tuning) and autotune sit in the Sample section and are applied to the sample itself, not to each lap, and the sample can be reversed. Transposing keeps the sample's length and the keys move with it. Autotune works differently on a sampler: it listens to the whole sample once, takes its pitch (after any transposing), and moves all of it by one fixed amount to the nearest note of the key and scale, scaled by Amount, so vibrato and slides stay as played; Speed does not apply. The keys then count from the tuned note. Every lap is rendered from the processed sample, so a change is heard on all of them, and recording a new sample keeps the laps already played.
- **Envelope.** The volume over the length of the sample, drawn over its waveform like the graphical EQ, with the shaped waveform shown bright over the original. Drag **A** sideways for the attack, from nothing to 2 seconds, for a swell; **D** sideways for the decay and up or down for the sustain level, so a short decay to a low sustain gives a sharper attack; and **R**, in the box on the right, for the release: how long a Held note fades after you let go. **Punch** lifts the first moment after the attack above the recorded level, by up to +9.5 dB, falling back over 30 ms, for more attack than the recording has. Double-tap a handle to reset it. The envelope is applied to the sample after pitch and autotune, so it is heard live on the pads and on every lap, and a new sample recorded on the track keeps it. A note cannot last longer than the recording: the sustain holds at most to the end of the trimmed sample.
- **Compressor** for the sample, after the envelope settings: On, Threshold, Ratio (up to ∞:1), Attack, Release and Gain. It evens the sample out, pulling the loud parts down, and then brings the whole sample back to its own loudness, so the body and tail come forward; it shows how far the loudest part is pulled down. Because the sample is processed in advance it looks ahead: the gain starts coming down Attack before a loud part, so no peak slips through and the peaks stay within full scale. It comes after pitch and autotune and before the envelope, so the envelope you draw shapes the compressed sound (Punch then gives a harder attack). Saved with the project and kept when a new sample is recorded on the track or copied to a new track.
- **Notes:** Full plays the whole sample on every note; Held stops it when you let go, fading over the envelope's release. Chosen on the pads.
- **Fine trim:** Start and End sliders in the envelope view move the trim edges within 150 ms of where they were, a sample at a time; double-tap either for where Auto trim puts it. **Reverse** sits with the trim view.
- **The pads** come up from the bottom when you tap a sampler's keys button on the play screen. Tabs along the top switch between the sampler tracks, and **+ Sampler** makes a new one (the first empty track, or a new track) and offers to record its sample right there. Below them are **Rec lap**, which records that track from its next lap (it reads Cancel while waiting, and Finish or Keep going while you record with the Record button), **Full** or **Held** notes, and **Octaves**, from 0 to 4: at 0 the pads are **one big pad** that plays the sample as recorded, otherwise **a keyboard** of that many octaves stacked in rows, highest at the top, with the root note marked; **From** moves it up or down an octave at a time. A sample with no clear pitch starts on the big pad. Every finger plays its own note and sliding along the keys plays each one. The transport stays at the bottom; swipe down, tap the play view above the sheet, or tap Done to close it.
- **Recording what you play.** Rec lap, or the track's ● button, records one lap of what you play on the screen, from its next lap. With the pad screen open, Record records lap after lap into the sampler, each lap a take on top of the last (or replacing it, with Overdub off), and laps with nothing played are thrown away; Capture last keeps the lap you just played. While a recording is on (waiting for its lap, counting in or recording) the pads are silent, and framed in red, since anything heard live would be late; each note is placed at its touch time minus the screen latency and the lap plays in time from the next lap on. Otherwise the pads sound at once, to try the sample out.
- **New track with this sample**, under a sampler's name at the top of Edit, makes a new track with the same sample and all its settings, without the laps.
- Each lap keeps the screen latency it was recorded with and can be nudged afterwards, like a take. The sample and the notes of every lap are saved with the project.

## Graphical EQ

Each track and the master have a three-band EQ drawn as its response curve. Drag the L, M or H handle sideways to set the frequency and up or down to set the level, from −12 to +12 dB; double-tap a handle to return it to its default level and frequency. The low and high bands are shelves and the mid band is a bell. The curve is the real response of the filters, the filter setting included.

## Scenes

Five scene buttons, A to E, sit just above the five transport buttons. Hold one to store which tracks are muted and soloed; tap it to bring that back. By default a scene takes effect when the lap you are hearing ends, so you can pick the next part ahead; its button blinks until then. Under Settings, Scenes, it can instead act immediately or on the next bar. A scene that matches the current state lights up. Scenes are saved with the project.

## Master effects

Under Master in the Edit view:

- Volume, graphical EQ and filter.
- Delay: time as 1/16, 1/8, dotted 1/8 or 1/4 note, or free in milliseconds; repeats, level and mix send.
- Reverb: size, level and mix send.
- Compressor: threshold and amount. The click goes through a matching neutral compressor, so click and tracks are delayed by the same few milliseconds and timing is unaffected.

## Projects

- **Everything is saved.** The first recording creates a project named after the date and time, and every change after that is saved automatically: tracks, takes, track settings, scenes, loop length, tempo and master effects. Rename it under Settings. After a reload, a Continue button reopens the last session.
- The raw recordings are stored, not the finished loops, so the latency of every take can still be changed after a project is reopened.
- Projects are stored in the browser on the device (IndexedDB) and are not synced anywhere.
- **Performance recording.** Records everything you hear, without the click, as an audio file. With the microphone included, what you play live is in it too, lined up with the loops by delaying the music by the latency. Recordings are kept under Settings, where you can save or share them.
- Export as a WAV file of the audible tracks, without effects or pan. With tracks of different lengths, the export runs until they all line up again.

## Live use

- The screen stays awake while playing. Large transport buttons: Start, Undo, Record, Capture last and Click on or off.
- Keys, and Bluetooth pedals that send them: <kbd>Space</kbd>, <kbd>R</kbd>, <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>→</kbd> work like the Record button (tap to record, hold to start or stop), <kbd>←</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd> undo, <kbd>C</kbd> capture last, <kbd>K</kbd> click, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd> mute a track and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>9</kbd> solo it, <kbd>E</kbd> edit, <kbd>S</kbd> settings, <kbd>Esc</kbd> back to play, <kbd>P</kbd> performance recording.

## Technical notes

Everything lives in `index.html`, with no build step and no dependencies apart from two web fonts. Recording runs in an AudioWorklet that writes the microphone into a ring buffer indexed by the audio clock, which is what makes sample-accurate alignment possible. The page must be served over https for the microphone to work. `sw.js` makes the app work offline.

A touch on the screen is placed on the audio clock from the event's own time stamp, through the offset between `performance.now()` and the audio clock's `currentTime`. `currentTime` moves once per audio buffer, so a reading is never ahead of the true offset, only behind it; the freshest reading of the last second is used. `getOutputTimestamp()` is not used, since its offset on a phone with Bluetooth is not known to stay put. Any fixed offset left in the mapping is part of the screen latency measured by tapping, so it cancels out. The sampler keeps a log of these events, and a lap of notes collects from it everything played in its window, like a recording reads the microphone ring.

The transposer and the autotune share one engine: pitch detection with YIN, and a phase vocoder with phase locking whose ratio can change over time, processed as a loop so the seam stays smooth, followed by resampling; it runs in a Web Worker. Alignment, measurement, the gestures, the EQ, the transposer, the sampler, projects and the track features are verified with automated tests in Chrome using simulated audio.
